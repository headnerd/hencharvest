# PROTOTYPE — ONE TUESDAY (rev 2)

A full day, written out as it would read. Now includes the post-ticket **verdict**, the
**catch-all misfiling** branch, and **probation**.

Format: a scene presents 2–4 **filing options**, the player picks one, the world reacts in
three to six lines, a **verdict** is issued, and the day moves on. Tuesday: ~7 minutes.

**How to read the bold lines** — those are the only UI. No meter bar, no notification, no
tooltip. The player reads three numbers and gets on with it.

**Rev 2 note:** verdicts and the misfiling branch are new. Read scene 4 and the day-8 beat
even if you skip the rest — those are what need testing. `BRANCHING.md` has the full
six-week state ledger and the rules this file is checked against.

## SCENE 1 — THE CHOSEN ONE IS ON TIME

He is not early. He is on time. The tragedy is that the fortress has learned to schedule
around him, which means the schedule is now *accommodating the hero*, which means everyone
knows, without discussing it, that the hero works here.

> A sound from the courtyard. Not an attack. Worse: a *person*, arriving, on the date
> expected.
>
> The Chosen One has entered the premises. He was carrying a +3 longsword and a grievance.
> I have logged both. The sword is in Lost & Found. The grievance is with Legal.
>
> He says he is here for the thing at the bottom. The thing at the bottom is a filing
> cabinet. It was the thing at the bottom in March.

### Choose a filing category:

**1. SCHEDULING CONFLICT** — He is here on a Tuesday. Tuesdays are a standing conflict.

**2. SAFETY COMPLIANCE** — He is near the pit trap. The trap is not a person and cannot
consent to this proximity.

## SCENE 1 RESOLUTION — *1, SCHEDULING CONFLICT*

> The notice went out. He received it. He is required to be notified of a conflict, and now
> he has been notified of a conflict, and the conflict is that he is here.
>
> He is upset about the notice. He is not upset about the notice. He is upset that the
> notice *worked* — that he was, briefly, at 10:14, indisputably a scheduling problem
> rather than a man with a sword.
>
> He is now, technically, still a scheduling problem. But he is a scheduling problem who is
> aware that we have paperwork, and this is a new and worse category of threat.

> **VERDICT: ACCEPTED.** The matter is closed. No follow-up required.

**PATIENCE +1 · CAREER +1**

## SCENE 2 — THE DRAGON'S REVIEW

The dragon is in the amphitheatre. He is early. He is always early for reviews and never
early for anything else, because the review is the only appointment where he is the subject
instead of the predator.

> The dragon has arrived ahead of schedule. He is seated. He is holding a copy of the
> previous review, which he has reconstructed from memory, which means the reconstruction is
> better than the original.
>
> He has prepared. I would like it noted that he has prepared.

> "I want to be better. I want to be better and I do not know what I am doing wrong and I
> have thought about it every day for a year."
>
> He is not asking for a raise. He is asking for the thing where the problem is named.

**No verdict on this scene.** A performance review is not a filing. It does not resolve and
it does not get rejected. It just happens, and then it is over, and he never finds out how it
went — which is, on reflection, the most realistic review system ever built.

### Choose a review outcome:

**1. NAME THE PROBLEM** — Close the incubator. Give him the one thing he asked for.
*He will cry. He will also comply. Both are permanent.*

**2. REDIRECT** — The problem is not the dragon. The problem is the incubator's signage.
*Safe. Devastating. He will spend the next week finding out you were right.*

**3. EXTEND THE CYCLE** — Push the review to next quarter.
*He has been told this before. He will accept it. He will remember it.*

**4. LOBSTER** — A gesture claw as a performance bonus. Entirely meaningless. He is deeply
moved.
*Costs nothing. Buys you a week. Do not do this two weeks running.*

## SCENE 3 — 16:40, THE PIT TRAP

The scene the game is named after, and it is a *paperwork* scene, and the only dangerous
thing in it is a signature.

> The pit trap is functional. I want to state that plainly, because the file says otherwise
> and the file is written by people who were not here.
>
> The pit trap is functional. The file says "non-compliant." The file is not wrong, exactly.
> The file is *thorough*, and thoroughness is a different problem than compliance, and only
> one of them is my responsibility.

**PATIENCE 4/10** — he has asked about this twice. The third time is the memo.

> The form requires a signature from a manager with authority over the hazard.
>
> You are a manager with authority over the hazard.
>
> I would like to draw your attention to the fact that these are the same.

### Choose a resolution:

**1. SIGN AS-IS** — The trap is documented. The trap remains. Everyone is content and
nothing has changed.
*This is the intended outcome of the entire game.*

**2. SIGN AND ADD A SAFETY CLAUSE** — Four extra lines. Genuinely safer. The trap is now
*conditioned*, and a conditioned trap misses one hero in five.
*Costs three patience. Buys you a year of Tuesdays.*

**3. SIGN IT SAFE** — The trap is now a safety feature. It will not kill.
*CAREER +2. The Dark Lord will not speak to you directly again.*

**4. REFER IT UP** — Send the form to the Dark Lord himself.
*He will sign it. He will sign it correctly. It will contain one line you will think about
for a long time.*

## DAY 8, LATER — WHAT THE PROMOTION LOOKS LIKE

*This branch assumes the player has already taken a strike (STRIKES 1/3, TITLE: Regional
Manager Deputy) and is at MORALE 4, CAREER 3/10. The misfiling only pays when you are
demoted — at full title it costs you three meters and promotes you to the title you already
have. See `BRANCHING.md` week 4→6 for the full run.*

The memo arrives in the morning. It is not addressed to you, which is unusual, and it is not
about you, which is the first time that has been true.

> **Re:** Filing quality
> Your filing is not compliant with the category definitions. This is the third such filing
> this quarter.
>
> Continue as you are.
>
> *There is no signature. There has never needed to be one. The letterhead is a courtesy.*

> Your title has been updated. The effective date is today. No meeting has been scheduled
> and none is required.
>
> The filing quality concern raised on the 14th is considered closed.

**TITLE: Regional Manager (Deputy)** → **TITLE: Regional Manager** · **MORALE 4 → 2** · **CAREER 3 → 5**

Then three mornings of nothing much happening.

> Tuesday's filing has been returned.
>
> This is the second such return this month. The previous one is also being returned, for
> completeness, as it was outstanding.
>
> No reason has been provided for either.

**PROBATION: CAREER 5 → 4 → 3 → 2** over three days. No memo. The player is simply worse at
their job for a while and cannot stop it.

> *Goblin 4:* Was something said to you?
> *You:* About the title?
> *Goblin 4:* About anything. You look like someone said something.

**The player now knows.** Being bad at the job is the only thing that has ever worked for
them, and there is no memo anywhere in this fortress that says so.

## THE READ

What this prototype is *for*. Five things, in priority order.

1. **Does the misfiling promotion work without being explained?** This is the only real
   test. Play scene 1 option 4 three Tuesdays running, watch the title change, and see
   whether you connect it. If you connect it *immediately*, the verdict text is too clear
   and the discovery is dead. If you never connect it, it reads as a bug. The window is
   narrow and it is the whole game.
2. **The Chosen One never appears doing anything cool.** He is a filing problem from the
   first line to the last. I'd defend that hard — his power is that he is *unfixable as a
   process*, and the moment he becomes a boss fight, the game stops being about management.
3. **The dragon scene is the one real person in the game.** It's the only moment the
   register could have gone sentimental, and it goes somewhere worse instead. Note that the
   review gets **no verdict** — it's not a filing, it just happens to him, and he never
   finds out how it went.
4. **Everything resolves into a filing.** Even the pit trap. The strongest ending state is
   "the paperwork is perfect and the trap still works."
5. **The mutual exclusion on day 8 is deliberate.** A player who spent Morale misfiling
   arrives at the goblins' ask with no Morale to spend. The branches are exclusive by
   arithmetic, not by script. Check that it doesn't feel like a closed door.

**One open worry.** The catch-all currently looks like a trap for new players — it's the
fourth option, it's always wrong, and it costs three meters. A first-run player will learn
to never touch it, which is correct behaviour and exactly the opposite of what the
promotion needs. That tension is real and I don't know yet how to solve it. Possibly the
verdict should occasionally make the catch-all look *tempting* for reasons that are also
wrong. Flagging it rather than pretending I've handled it.
